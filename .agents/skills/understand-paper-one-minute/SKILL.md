# Understand this paper in one minute

## Input

Use the paper itself or a lawful, user-provided text extraction. If only metadata or an abstract is available, say so prominently and limit claims to that source. Identify the paper with its stable project ID when available.

## Method

1. Locate the research question, population or dataset, imaging modality and acquisition protocol, method, comparator, evaluation design, primary metric, and main result.
2. Check the paper's tables and figures for the result rather than relying on the abstract alone. Record page, table, or figure references for factual claims.
3. Separate what the authors report from your interpretation. Keep dataset-specific results tied to their dataset and evaluation design. Preserve units and uncertainty intervals when given.
4. Mark anything absent or unclear as `not reported` or `unclear`; never infer a missing protocol, external validation, or code availability.

## Output

Aim for 120–160 words, readable in about one minute:

- **Question:** The problem and why it matters.
- **Approach:** What the authors did, in plain language.
- **Evidence:** Dataset, modality, comparator, evaluation design, and one or two decisive numbers with provenance.
- **Takeaway:** The narrow conclusion supported by those results.
- **Caveat:** The most consequential limitation or missing test.
- **Source:** Paper ID and precise page/table/figure references; state if the assessment used only an abstract.

Define unfamiliar terms in a few words. Do not turn a single study into a clinical recommendation.
